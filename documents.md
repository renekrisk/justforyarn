
PROSPECTS Kenya: Digital local services marketplace for Refugee and Host Community Youth in Turkana and Garissa
Terms of Reference
Country: Kenya
Duty station / operational coverage: Turkana and Garissa Counties
Type of engagement: Service Contract

The International Labour Organization (ILO) is seeking to engage a qualified firm (for-profit) to support the roll out of digital local services marketplace interventions under the PROSPECTS programme in Kenya. The assignment will focus on facilitating access to decent work opportunities for refugee and host community youth in Turkana and Garissa counties through digital local services marketplaces.

Interested for-profit firms should submit their technical and financial proposals by 10 July 2026, 23:59 EAT, quoting “SC-Digital Local Services Marketplace– PROSPECTS Kenya.”

•	A profile of the firm highlighting institutional capacity, relevant experience, staffing, and examples of similar assignments.
•	Evidence of existing job pipelines, client contracts, delivery projects, employer networks, marketplace activity, client demand, or other relevant demand-side evidence.
•	A financial proposal with a clear cost breakdown by deliverable and milestone.

1.	Background 
1.1 Overview
Kenya continues to face significant youth labour market challenges. Employment creation has not kept pace with the growth in the working-age population, and young people continue to experience disproportionately high levels of unemployment, underemployment, informality, and poor job quality. These challenges are more severe for young women, for youth in historically marginalized areas, and for populations with limited access to labour market information, employment services, and professional networks.
Forced displacement adds another layer of complexity. Kenya hosts a large refugee population, with the majority residing in Turkana and Garissa counties, particularly in and around Kakuma and Dadaab. Refugees and host communities in these counties face overlapping constraints, including poverty, fragile local labour markets, weak private sector presence, limited formal employment opportunities, and restricted access to quality livelihood options. While humanitarian and development partners have invested in skills, livelihoods, and entrepreneurship initiatives, the transition from training to actual employment remains inadequate.
At the same time, Kenya’s digital economy continues to expand. Digitalization has created opportunities in software development, digital support services, business process functions, content production, design, online service delivery, platform-enabled business functions, and other digitally enabled occupations. The growth of remote and distributed work models also creates possibilities for participation by workers located outside major urban centres. In principle, this could reduce some of the geographic disadvantages faced by youth in refugee-hosting counties.
Digitalization is also reshaping access to local service markets. In Kenya, digital intermediation models are increasingly being used to connect workers and service providers to clients, customers, and market opportunities. These models can expand visibility, strengthen access to work opportunities, support transitions into paid work, and create a practical entry point for integrating skills recognition, upskilling, tooling and retooling, mentorship, soft skills development, work-based learning, and gradual alignment with decent work principles.
However, these opportunities are not automatically inclusive. Without deliberate intervention, digital labour markets can reproduce or deepen inequality. Access to decent digital work is constrained by several factors, including uneven internet connectivity, limited employer confidence in remote workers from marginalized locations, weak matching systems, poor access to professional networks, and the growth of low-value or exploitative work arrangements. Access to work opportunities through digitally mediated local service markets can likewise be constrained by weak platform practices, limited visibility of qualified providers, low client trust, unequal access to digital tools, and the absence of systems that support fairness, transparency, accountability, and worker protection.
In many cases, programmes marketed as digital jobs interventions have focused on training without demonstrating demand, or have linked participants to fragmented, low-paid, task-based work that does not meet decent work standards.
The ILO’s comparative advantage in this space lies in supporting access to decent work. For the purposes of this assignment, this means work opportunities that are structured, remunerated in a predictable and fair manner, and linked to meaningful work outputs, supervision, accountability, and worker protection. It also means moving beyond training as an end in itself and focusing on actual labour market insertion and measurable work outcomes.
1.2 Operational context of the assignment
In response to the challenges facing both host communities and refugees, a partnership initiative titled: PROSPECTS - Partnership for improving Prospects for host communities and forcibly displaced persons’ , funded by the Government of the Netherlands and jointly implemented by IFC, ILO, UNHCR, UNICEF, and the World Bank, the ILO leads on employment and labour market systems interventions. In Kenya, PROSPECTS aims to improve sustainable living conditions for refugees and host communities by increasing access to livelihoods and employment in safe and decent work.
Within this framework, ILO has supported interventions related to digital skills, employment services, career guidance, and transition-to-work models for refugees and host communities in Turkana and Garissa. These efforts have generated valuable lessons. A central lesson is that employability support alone is insufficient where partners do not have direct access to real labour demand or credible market demand. Another is that many conventional providers struggle to deliver employment outcomes in remote counties. A third lesson is that where multiple partners operate at once, duplication in targeting can reduce efficiency and undermine programme credibility.
For this reason, ILO intends to engage a firm that operate digital local services marketplaces - platforms that connect clients or customers with local service providers through digital intermediation. This refers to platforms that connect clients or customers with workers such as artisans, technicians, home service providers, resellers, beauty workers, creative service providers, and other location-service-based workers through profile-based digital intermediation and client selection.
The firm should be able to demonstrate one or more of the following characteristics:
•	They have an operational digital platform that enables individuals or service providers to create profiles, showcase their skills or services, and connect with clients or customers. 
•	They facilitate client discovery, provider selection, service visibility, and access to paid work opportunities through digital intermediation. 
•	They have an existing or emerging user base, client base, marketplace activity, service demand, or commercial demand relevant to the target groups under this assignment. 
•	They are able to onboard, activate, and support participants, including refugees and host community members, to strengthen profile quality, visibility, customer engagement, and access to work opportunities. 
•	They are able to support or facilitate skills recognition, upskilling, tooling and retooling, mentorship, soft skills development, and work-based learning. 
•	They are willing and able to strengthen platform practices in line with decent work principles. 
The selected firm will therefore be engaged to identify, onboard, activate, support, and track participants on their platform to access paid work opportunities.


2.	Objective 
The overall objective of this assignment is to increase access to decent work opportunities for refugee and host community youth in Turkana and Garissa through digital local services marketplace.

The ILO seeks to engage a firm to support the transition of up to 200 youth, previously trained by other institutions, into sustainable local and international work opportunities, including remote and digitally enabled work.

The assignment will focus on demand-driven approaches that combine assessment, matching, activation or placement, and retention support.

Specifically, the firm will:
•	Assess participant readiness and align skills with labour market or marketplace demand. 
•	Provide targeted employability support where necessary to enable access to work opportunities. 
•	Facilitate access to work through activation in digital service marketplaces. 
•	Provide mentorship, job search support, and follow-up to strengthen retention and progression. 
•	Engage employers, clients, and platforms to secure and sustain opportunities. 
•	Leverage partnerships with government, private sector, and digital platforms to expand access to work opportunities.
3.	Scope of the Assignment 
The selected firm will be expected to deliver an end-to-end market access service that begins with verified marketplace demand or credible client demand and ends with measurable activation and retention outcomes. The work should be practical, demand-driven, and adapted to the realities of Turkana and Garissa.
The assignment will include, but will not necessarily be limited to, the following components:
3.1 Inception, validation of demand, and operational planning
At the start of the assignment, the selected firm will be expected to undertake an inception phase to confirm the feasibility of its proposed approach, validate the marketplace demand it intends to mobilize, and agree with ILO on implementation arrangements.
This phase should result in a clear operational plan covering:
•	The categories of service and work opportunities to be targeted, such as artisan services, home-based services, creative and freelance services, repair and maintenance services, resale opportunities, beauty and wellness services, digital profile-based services, and other relevant service categories accessible through the platform. 
•	The source of the labour demand or market demand, including existing clients, consulting projects, framework agreements, outsourcing mandates, employer networks, managed delivery contracts, platform users, client traffic, service requests, and commercial partnerships. 
•	The minimum qualification profile for each job category or service category. 
•	The expected number of placements, activations, or paid work opportunities by county, gender, and category. 
•	The operational modality through which participants will access and perform work, including whether opportunities are platform-mediated, delivered through direct client engagement on the platform, hybrid, or through other arrangements consistent with the platform model. 
•	The tools, systems, and management processes that will be used to supervise and support participants. 
ILO expects this phase to go beyond generic statements of intent. Applicants should demonstrate that the opportunities they propose to facilitate are linked to real platform activity or credible client demand rather than speculative pipeline development.
Deliverable 1: Submission of a comprehensive inception report outlining the implementation approach, validated demand (labour or marketplace), target participant allocation, delivery model, staffing, risk mitigation measures, and detailed workplan aligned to the assignment. Timeline: Within 30 days of contract signature

3.3 Candidate assessment, matching, and pre-placement support
Once participants are pre-selected, the selected firm will be responsible for assessing candidates against available roles or opportunities and determining their suitability for activation.

This should include:
•	Review of candidate profiles, portfolios, prior work, assessments, and other evidence of capability. 
•	Role-specific or opportunity-specific screening using methods appropriate to the job or opportunity type, such as tests, simulations, task reviews, portfolio assessments, interviews, communications screening, project-fit discussions, service-readiness assessment, or profile quality review. 
•	Identification of any limited employability gaps that can be addressed through short onboarding or reinforcement support rather than long-form training. 

The assignment is NOT designed as a training contract. However, the selected firm may provide targeted pre-activation support where this is necessary to secure successful platform participation or paid work opportunities. This support may include:
•	Professional communication and remote work etiquette 
•	Time management and task organization 
•	Workplace discipline and accountability 
•	Team collaboration and client communication 
•	Familiarization with tools and systems required for the role or platform 
•	Mentorship and orientation to work culture 
•	Customer handling 
•	Digital profile improvement 
•	Pricing and negotiation support 
•	Platform use and response management 
•	Skills recognition support 
•	Upskilling support linked to market demand 
•	Tooling and retooling support relevant to service delivery or work readiness 
•	Mentorship 
•	Soft skills development 

Such support must be practical, time-bound, and directly linked to placement, paid work opportunities, improved marketplace participation, service quality, or progression outcomes. The selected firm should not use this assignment to substitute broad-based training for actual market access outcomes.

Deliverable 3: Submission of a report detailing participant assessment outcomes, shortlisted candidates, onboarding processes, and readiness for placement or marketplace activation, including targeted support provided. Timeline: Within 90 days of contract signature


3.4 Marketplace activation and access to paid work opportunities
The core of the assignment is the activation of participants on the digital local services marketplace into measurable paid work opportunities. The firm engaged under this assignment may facilitate this through different pathways, provided the opportunities meet the required standards and the pathway is clearly explained in the proposal. Acceptable pathways may include:
•	Activation of participants on digital marketplaces that generate verified client engagements, paid assignments, repeat business, or other demonstrable marketplace outcomes. 
•	Platform-mediated opportunities where clients or customers independently select providers through profile-based listings and the platform supports access, transparency, and accountability. 

Work opportunities facilitated through the platform under this assignment must meet minimum decent work standards appropriate to the platform model. At a minimum, the firm will be expected to demonstrate that their platform:
•	Displays transparent pricing or pricing ranges to service providers before they accept a job, so workers can make informed decisions about which opportunities to take. 
•	Does not apply commission rates or platform fees at levels that make provider earnings unviable and discloses fee structures clearly to all service providers on the platform. 
•	Operates a fair, transparent, and contestable rating and review system that does not unreasonably disadvantage providers and gives providers the ability to respond to or dispute client feedback. 
•	Has a clear and accessible grievance and dispute resolution mechanism through which service providers can raise concerns about clients, payments, unfair deactivation, or other platform-related issues, and receives a response within a reasonable timeframe. 
•	Does not deactivate or remove service providers arbitrarily or without notice and provides a clear process through which providers can understand the reasons for any deactivation and seek reconsideration. 
•	Does not lock service providers into exclusivity arrangements or prevent them from working across multiple platforms or through other channels and allows providers to access and take their own data, reviews, and work history with them. 
•	Generates or is credibly expected to generate sufficient job volume or client demand to make participation worthwhile for onboarded providers and does not onboard more providers than the platform’s current or near-term demand can reasonably sustain. 

Deliverable 4: Activation of participants on the Digital Local Services Marketplace with evidence of paid work opportunities, client engagements, and marketplace outcomes, supported by monthly progress reports. Timeline: Months 4 to 10:

3.5 Onboarding, supervision, and performance support
Following activation, the firm will be expected to support participants through the critical early stage of their engagement on the platform. This is necessary not only to improve retention but also to ensure that platform users and clients maintain confidence in the quality of providers.
The firm’s responsibilities at this stage should include:
•	Formal onboarding of participants to the role, team, tools, platform, and performance expectations. 
•	Assignment of supervisors, mentors, or focal persons for day-to-day support. 
•	Monitoring of attendance, participation, quality of work, responsiveness, platform activity, or service delivery quality, as relevant. 
•	Troubleshooting of common operational issues such as connectivity constraints, misunderstanding of tasks, communication breakdowns, workflow bottlenecks, profile visibility challenges, low client response, and service fulfilment issues. 
•	Regular follow-up with both the participant and the work recipient or client, where applicable. 

Given the target geography, applicants should explain how provider support will function under platform-mediated conditions and what practical measures they will use to maintain provider activity and quality.

Deliverable 5: Submission of retention and performance reports demonstrating continued participation, work continuity, progression, and earnings outcomes, including analysis of challenges and corrective actions. Timeline: Months 6 to 12 (reported quarterly)

3.6 Retention, progression, and post-placement tracking
ILO is interested not only in activation but also in retention and progression. The firm will therefore be expected to remain engaged beyond the initial activation stage and monitor whether participants continue securing paid work opportunities or remain active on the platform in a meaningful way. This will include:
•	Tracking participants at agreed milestones, including at least the first, third, and sixth month after placement or activation, where applicable. 
•	Recording retention outcomes, activity status, earnings status, and reasons for drop-off where participants leave or become inactive. 
•	Supporting participants and employers or clients to address early-stage challenges affecting continuity. 
•	Identifying opportunities for contract renewal, progression, repeat service engagements, higher-value opportunities, skills recognition, upskilling, tooling and retooling, mentorship, soft skills strengthening, or transition into more stable and higher-value work where possible. 

The firm should not treat activation as complete on the day a participant joins the platform. The contract is intended for sustained marketplace outcomes, not only initial onboarding.

Deliverable 6: Submission of a final report summarizing total participants reached, assessed, placed or activated, and retained; analysis of results achieved; lessons learned; and recommendations for sustainability and scale. Timeline: End of Month 12.


3.7 Inclusion Targets and Equity Requirements
To ensure alignment with the PROSPECTS programme’s commitment to inclusive and equitable outcomes, the selected firm will be required to meet minimum inclusion targets in their activation results. Specifically:
•	At least 40% of participants placed or activated must be women 
•	At least 40% of participants placed or activated must be refugees 
•	At least 5% of participants placed or activated must be persons with disabilities, where feasible and in line with reasonable accommodation practices 

The service provider is expected to:
•	Integrate inclusion considerations into their targeting, assessment, placement, activation, and support processes 
•	Demonstrate how they will address barriers to participation for women, refugees, and persons with disabilities 
•	Track and report on inclusion outcomes throughout implementation

8.	Evaluation Criteria
Proposals will be evaluated on the basis of technical quality and financial competitiveness. The technical evaluation will place strong emphasis on the credibility and health of the platform, the realism of the onboarding and activation approach, the quality of platform practices for service providers, and the applicant’s demonstrated ability to serve marginalised communities. Proposals will be scored out of 100 as follows:

•	Platform viability and existing client demand (30 points): Evidence that the platform is operational and generating real client and service demand, not a prototype or aspirational model. Evaluators will consider the volume and frequency of transactions or bookings, the size and activity of the client base, evidence of repeat usage, and the geographic coverage of the platform relative to Turkana and Garissa. 
•	Realism and quality of the onboarding and activation approach (25 points); clarity and practicality of how the applicant will identify, screen, onboard, and activate participants from the pre-identified pool onto the platform; how profile quality and visibility will be supported; how participants will be helped to access their first client engagements; and how the applicant will manage the risk of onboarding more providers than the platform’s demand can sustain. 
•	Quality of platform practices and provider protections (15 points): The extent to which the platform demonstrates fair, transparent, and provider-friendly operating practices. Evaluators will assess pricing transparency, commission or fee structures, the fairness and contestability of the rating system, grievance and dispute mechanisms, deactivation policies, and whether the platform avoids locking providers into exclusivity. Applicants should provide evidence of current practices, not stated intentions. 
•	Suitability of the platform for refugee and host community youth in Turkana and Garissa (20 points): The degree to which the platform’s service categories, geographic reach, language accessibility, device and connectivity requirements, and onboarding processes are compatible with the realities faced by participants in Kakuma and Dadaab. Evaluators will consider whether the platform has existing or credible plans for presence in these locations, and whether the types of services mediated by the platform are ones that participants can realistically provide and that clients in or near those areas are likely to demand. 
•	Inclusion by design (10 points): The degree to which the platform’s design, onboarding processes, and operating model actively support the participation of women, refugees, and persons with disabilities, not as an add-on, but as a built-in feature. Evaluators will look for concrete evidence of how the platform has served or intends to serve these groups, what barriers it has identified and addressed, and how it tracks and reports on provider demographics and outcomes.

TEXT BY AUNTI JANET AND MOM ABOUT THIS
At INK Coaching, we have had the privilege of participating in the implementation of the SANARA Program, a major youth empowerment initiative in Kenya that connects the creative and cultural industries with sustainable business enterprise. Inspired by the Swahili words sanaa (arts) and biashara (business), SANARA is backed by the Mastercard Foundation and implemented by a consortium of organizations including the Sanara – HEVA Fund.The program places a strong emphasis on inclusion, specifically targeting women, refugees, and persons with disabilities in both urban and rural communities. Through our role within this ecosystem, INK Coaching has contributed to youth training, professional development, and employment readiness support aimed at strengthening pathways to sustainable livelihoods and work opportunities.

Building on the lessons, partnerships, and impact achieved through SANARA, we are have developed an initiative called the VUKA Youth Connect Program. The proposed program is envisioned as a 28-month initiative designed to create a sustainable and inclusive ecosystem that empowers marginalized youth in Kenya to participate competitively in the digital economy.

Our goal is to bridge the gap between emerging African tech talent and the growing demand for digital skills in European markets, while promoting inclusive economic growth and cross-border innovation.

The program will specifically target youth between the ages of 18–35, including Persons with Disabilities (PWDs) and refugees living in marginalized areas across Kenya.

The VUKA Youth Connect Program will be implemented in four key phases:

Phase 1 – Talent Identification
This phase will focus on identifying high-potential youth from underserved and marginalized communities through community outreach, partnerships, and localized engagement initiatives.

Phase 2 – Digital Skills Enhancement
Selected participants will undergo intensive training aimed at strengthening digital competencies, remote work preparedness, employability skills, communication, and professional development aligned with international market demands.

Phase 3 – Employer Matching & Entrepreneurship Incubation
Participants will receive remote work readiness support and be connected to potential employers and virtual work opportunities. In addition, youth interested in entrepreneurship will undergo incubation support, mentorship, and business development training, culminating in an opportunity to pitch their ventures for potential funding and investment consideration.

Phase 4 – Sustainability Through the VUKA Platform
To ensure long-term impact and ecosystem sustainability, we intend to establish the VUKA Platform as a continuous support and engagement hub connecting youth to opportunities, mentorship, employers, investors, trainers, and peer learning networks.
